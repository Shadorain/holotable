# Holotable Intel

Intel is the board's fourth view: the **Intel** toggle button, key `4` (or `i`), or **Intel** under More. It keeps the board's header, picker, More, settings and inbox; switching to and from it is instant. Two sub-tabs, **Selected operation** and **Fleet overview**, share the same layout: headline cards, then the **Internal affairs** docket — a ranked list of open findings synthesized from the deterministic rules and rollup classes. Each case names the measured stat, what it puts at stake (`~2.3h · $4.10 at stake`), the worst offending contacts (clicking a chip opens that contact on the Board), and concrete fixes: copyable `/holotable` commands, Board or Plan map links, or manual checks. Operation cases rank by that stake; archives evaluated before stake ranking, and the fleet docket, rank by severity — critical actions first, then watches, then coverage caveats. An archive's chips and Board links are inert, since its contacts are no longer on the Board. **Selected operation** shows the operation in the header picker; while Intel is shown the picker also lists archived operations with their close date, and **Intel** under More on an open archive report shows that archive. Picking an operation switches to Selected operation; leaving Intel returns the picker to open operations. **Fleet overview** aggregates the same classes across evaluated sources. Each scope loads independently, so a failed fleet rollup cannot block an operation snapshot. Evidence loads on opening Intel, on a scope or source change, and on **Refresh snapshot**; there is no polling, live stream, chart library, new process or database. Counts read as whole numbers, rates with at most one decimal, shares as whole percents and money to the cent.

Operation headlines answer four questions: **Deliveries per active hour** (with the wall-time rate beside it, or the elapsed window under an hour), **Cost per landed contact** (with the share of launched contacts whose spend is fully known), **Waiting on you now** (open waits, and how many contacts they hold) and **Active time** against idle time. Below the docket:

- **Where time goes** — mission time against time waited on you, resolved by automation and still waiting, then one bar per wait kind: Plan approval, Pause gate, Decision, Question, Land, Launch and Resume.
- **Trends** — launches, deliveries, conflicts, review failures and the conflict rate per hourly, daily or weekly bucket; hovering a bar names its bucket and count.
- **Early vs recent** — the first third of landed work contacts by first launch against the last third (at least three each): median spend, active time and launch → land, relaunches, launch failures and review rounds per contact, and conflicts per land attempt, each with its direction of change. A long operation's fixed problems show here as a drop instead of staying in the totals.
- **Contacts** — one row per contact, sortable by lead, active, blocked and waited-on-you time, review rounds, spend and outcome; selecting a row opens the contact on the Board, and **Details** expands its timeline, blockers, waits, relaunches, conflicts and spend. Below 640 px the table keeps the contact, outcome and lead columns, sorts from a menu, and folds the rest into Details.
- **Spend** — landed work, integration and decisions, still open, abandoned and decision panes, with wasted spend (abandoned plus panes) highlighted and a lower-bound caveat when not every launched contact is fully priced.
- **Stalls** (quota holds, offline sockets, launch refusals by cause, review failures) and **Conflicts** (rate, most conflicting paths and contacts).
- **Phase ledger** — one row per phase with its share of measured time, attributed spend and tokens — and **Recorded evidence & coverage**, which folds every other scalar into a single drawer.

Archives evaluated before these breakdowns existed keep the older headline cards (delivered work, known spend, median delivery, check failures) and omit the panels they lack, saying so; current snapshots show the older figures as a totals row under the headlines. A selected archive that no longer exists falls back to the picker's operation with a notice. **Fleet overview** shows the same four headlines summed over the operations that report them (each card says across how many), the same Where time goes and Spend panels, and keeps its rankings, cohorts and diagnostics in on-demand drawers; nothing renders a second copy of data already in the docket. Empty panels say what fills them.

An operation's evaluation also answers the questions you ask of a running or finished operation (field reference: `docs/architecture.md`, Deterministic evaluation):

- **Each contact** — kind, state and outcome (landed, abandoned, open), when it was queued, launched and landed, lead and active time, blocked time before and after launch and by which tickets, phase split, review rounds with findings per round, relaunches by you versus Recovery, conflicts with their paths, and its spend (with whether that spend is fully known).
- **Waiting on you** — how long ready or gated work waited on an operator (launch, decision, plan approval, Pause gate, questions and tool prompts, Continue, Land), how much of that automation resolved instead, what is waiting now, and how long missions worked on their own.
- **Throughput and trends** — deliveries per active hour (stretches with nothing queued or live excluded) and hourly, daily or weekly counts of launches, deliveries, land attempts, conflicts and review failures.
- **Spend** — cost per landed contact from landed contacts' spend only, open spend, wasted spend (abandoned contacts and decision panes) and the known-cost share, alongside `partial`.
- **Stalls** — mission stalls split into quota, provider rate limits and other problems (timeouts, errors), offline time, how long auto mode waited for provider capacity and Recovery deferred restarts (session slots, memory, a refused restart), and failed launch refusals, apart from review failures. Histories recorded before the board kept the mission's problem count every quota hold, rate limits included, as quota.
- **Conflicts** — conflict rate and the most conflicting paths and contacts.

Recommendations are ranked by what they put at stake (time, or known spend converted to time at the operation's own spend rate), so waiting, delivery and dependency advice is never cut by the six-rule cap in favor of accounting caveats, and each cites its newest evidence. Once Early vs recent has cohorts, relaunch churn, review retries and repeated verification judge only contacts first launched since the recent third began, and their caveat says so: churn the operation has moved past stops being advised.

Fleet event ratios include observed zero-failure operations in their denominators. Time/cost shares use all recorded category totals, not just operations where that category appeared; overlapping waits are not shares of wall-clock time. Operation spend includes decision panes. Board-wide draft spend is separate and excluded from operation cost per delivery; the board-pane subtotal must not be added again to operation spend.
All evaluation uses recorded history with no additional model calls or model-context tokens; nothing changes approvals, execution or scheduling. Landing, self-checks and model reviews are not externally verified correctness. Cost is an associated estimate, not an invoice or subscription bill. Archived reports freeze evaluation at close; legacy reports without it show **Evaluation not recorded**. The optional controlled-comparison fold explains `holotable compare --manifest FILE`, explicit baselines and matched `maxWorkers` trials; it never schedules an experiment.

**Optional small-model interpretation:** **Export review prompt** downloads the current operation/archive or fleet snapshot as a bounded text prompt (maximum 32 KiB). It includes measurements, unknown/partial coverage and bounded recommendation/event references; it excludes raw transcripts and authentication headers. List/string omissions are declared, and oversized exports fail explicitly. Export is disabled while loading, after a failed scope switch, or when an archive has no evaluation; it never silently uses the previous scope.

Inspect the file, then explicitly run your chosen small model from its download directory:

```sh
omp --model YOUR_SMALL_MODEL --no-tools --no-extensions --no-skills --no-rules --no-title --no-session --max-time 2m -p @holotable-intel-review.txt
```

The prompt requests at most five evidence-citing findings, with hypotheses separated from measurements. This is tool-free advisory interpretation, not an agent independently inspecting code or changing missions. The time limit is not a price cap; choose your provider/model deliberately. This separate OMP session's charges are not automatically attributed to the operation. Intel itself makes no model call, changes no policy, and does not store model output as deterministic evidence.

The base header still shows operational `open/cap omp` and note task progress. Detailed analytics belong only in Intel.
